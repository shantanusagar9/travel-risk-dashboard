Best Time
March–May (spring) offers mild weather, fewer crowds, and ideal conditions for business travel and city exploration; September–October (early autumn) is similarly pleasant with lower temperatures than peak summer and reduced tourist congestion in major cities.

Spain is the world's second-most visited country, receiving over 96 million international visitors in 2025, with a highly developed economy, strong rule of law, and world-class infrastructure. It is a major hub for European business, financial services, technology, and tourism, making it an important destination for multinational corporate travellers.

Visa (Indian Passport): Indian passport holders require a Schengen short-stay visa (Type C) to enter Spain; no visa-on-arrival is available. The visa permits a maximum 90-day stay within any 180-day period and grants access to all 29 Schengen countries. Applications are processed via BLS International centres in India. Required documents include a passport valid for at least 3 months beyond departure, travel insurance with minimum €30,000 coverage, proof of accommodation, return flight booking, proof of funds, and employer NOC. Visa fee is approximately INR 8,920 plus a service charge.
📌 Key Features
  • Spain is a Schengen Area member; Indian passport holders require a Schengen visa prior to travel
  • Euro is the official currency; credit cards widely accepted, though cash remains useful in smaller establishments
  • High-speed AVE rail network connects major cities; Madrid and Barcelona have extensive metro systems
  • Spain has universal emergency number 112; English-speaking operators available in major cities

🛡️ Risk Assessment
✊ Civil Unrest Moderate

Demonstrations are common in Spain and may occur in response to political or economic issues, on politically significant holidays, and during international events. Anti-tourism protests have intensified in 2025–2026, particularly in Barcelona, Palma de Mallorca, San Sebastián, and Valencia, driven by soaring housing costs and overtourism. Mass housing protests occurred in Madrid as recently as May 2026, with thousands rallying over rental costs that rose nearly 13% year-on-year at end of 2025.

🔒 Crime Moderate

Pickpocketing and distraction-based theft are the most common crimes affecting business and leisure travellers in Spain, especially in Barcelona and Madrid. Barcelona recorded approximately 47,000 reported thefts in 2025, with foreign visitors accounting for the majority of victims. Car break-ins are also frequent; violent crime is lower but has been reported, and credit-card skimming and financial scams targeting foreigners remain concerns.

🎯 Terrorism Moderate

Terrorist groups continue plotting possible attacks in Spain, and may strike with little or no warning. The US State Department specifically flags tourist locations, transportation centres, markets, shopping malls, hotels, restaurants, places of worship, parks, and major sporting and cultural events as potential targets. Spanish authorities have taken strong preventive measures, including arresting suspects linked to plots, and have enhanced security at major attractions such as the Sagrada Família and the Alhambra.

👩 Women's Safety Low

Spain ranks as one of the safest countries in Europe for women travellers, with strong legal protections against discrimination and harassment. Solo female travel is generally safe, though standard urban precautions apply after dark, particularly in nightlife districts. A 5.7% increase in reported sexual freedom offences was noted in 2025 crime data, and travellers are advised to avoid isolated areas at night and not accept drinks from strangers.

  • Stay with a group or trusted companion during nighttime outings and avoid walking alone in poorly lit areas
  • Use licensed taxis or reputable ride-sharing apps rather than walking alone after dark in tourist-heavy areas
🏥 Health Low

Spain has a high-quality healthcare system with near-universal public coverage and a strong network of private hospitals serving international patients. No vaccinations are required for entry, though the CDC recommends ensuring routine immunisations are up to date, including MMR given rising global measles cases as of 2025. Tap water is safe to drink in most cities and regions.

💨 Air Pollution Low

Spain's overall air quality is generally good, with Madrid and Barcelona typically recording AQI levels in the 'Good' to low 'Moderate' range for most of the year. Urban traffic remains the primary pollution source; ozone levels can spike during summer heatwaves, and Saharan dust episodes periodically push particulate readings higher across southern regions.

AQI: 20–60 (Good to Moderate) in major cities; occasional spikes to 80+ during summer heatwaves or dust events · Peak: July–August (ozone/heat) and February (particulate episodes)

🚗 Road Safety Moderate

Road conditions in Spain can differ significantly from other countries; traffic in Madrid and Barcelona is fast-paced, with different signage, traffic light placement, and driving habits. Spain recorded 1,145 road deaths in 2025; rural mountain roads in the Pyrenees and Picos de Europa require particular caution, especially at night when farm animals and poorly marked roads pose additional hazards. An International Driving Permit is required for foreign nationals wishing to rent or drive a car.

🌪️ Natural Disasters Moderate

Spain experiences intense heatwaves in summer, particularly in Andalusia where temperatures can exceed 40°C (104°F), posing serious health risks for travellers engaged in outdoor activities. Flash flooding and severe thunderstorms are common in autumn on the Mediterranean coast — particularly in the Valencian Community and the Balearic Islands — and wildfires are an increasing risk during dry summer months across southern and central regions.

📰 Current Events
Civil Disturbance Housing Protest Wave: Thousands Rally in Madrid Over 13% Rent Surge Medium

Thousands of protesters gathered in central Madrid on 24 May 2026 demanding government action on housing costs, which rose nearly 13% year-on-year at end 2025. The Bank of Spain estimates a shortfall of 700,000 homes nationally. Business travellers should avoid the city centre during announced protest days and monitor local media for road closures and transport disruptions.

Security Worldwide Caution Alert: US Embassy Spain Issues Security Notice (June 2025) Medium

The US Embassy in Madrid issued a worldwide caution alert on 22 June 2025, citing the Israel-Iran conflict and the potential for demonstrations against US citizens and interests in Spain. Business travellers from all nationalities are advised to maintain a low profile, avoid large gatherings, and register with their home country's embassy travel alert system.

Natural Disaster April 2025 Iberian Peninsula Blackout: Grid Resilience Concerns Remain Medium

On 28 April 2025, the entire electricity grid of mainland Spain and Portugal collapsed in an unprecedented event, leaving millions without power for up to 24 hours and disrupting transport, communications, and emergency services. ENTSO-E's expert panel attributed the blackout to cascading voltage instability. A Spanish Senate inquiry is ongoing and grid upgrades are in progress, but the event has highlighted infrastructure vulnerabilities relevant to business continuity planning.

Civil Disturbance Anti-Tourism Protests Intensify Across Spain in 2025–2026 Low

Anti-tourism protests have escalated in Barcelona, San Sebastián, Palma de Mallorca, Valencia, and Granada, driven by housing shortages and overdevelopment. The US Embassy issued a demonstration alert specifically for these protests on 13 June 2025. Protests are largely peaceful but can cause localised disruption to roads and public transport; travellers in affected cities should allow extra travel time and avoid protest routes.

✅ Recommendations
1
Carry a certified photocopy of your passport at all times, as Spanish law requires foreigners to produce identification on demand; store the original securely at your hotel
2
Use anti-theft bags or money belts in crowded tourist areas such as Las Ramblas, Puerta del Sol, and major metro stations, and never leave valuables visible in rental cars
3
Avoid all demonstrations and large gatherings — housing and anti-tourism protests are ongoing and can escalate; monitor local news and US Embassy Madrid alerts before travel
4
Ensure comprehensive travel insurance with a minimum €30,000 medical cover (mandatory for Schengen visa) and confirm your insurer's 24/7 emergency line before departure
5
Book appointments in advance with English-speaking hospitals such as Quirónsalud or Ruber Internacional if medical treatment may be required; keep a list of emergency contacts
6
Prepare for potential infrastructure disruptions: carry sufficient cash (following the April 2025 blackout, ATMs and card terminals failed across the country) and keep mobile devices charged with a portable power bank
