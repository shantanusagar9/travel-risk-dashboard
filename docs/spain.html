Spain is one of Europe's leading economies and a premier global business destination, offering well-developed infrastructure, strong tourism and financial sectors, and easy access to both European and Latin American markets. The country recorded over 83 million international visitors in 2025, reflecting its robust hospitality and transport networks suited to corporate travellers.

Visa (Indian Passport): Indian passport holders require a Schengen Short-Stay Visa (Type C) to enter Spain — visa-on-arrival and visa-free entry are not available for Indian citizens. The visa permits stays of up to 90 days within any 180-day period and grants access to all 29 Schengen countries. Applications must be submitted in person through BLS International centres in India (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata); the visa fee is approximately €90 for adults. Processing typically takes 15 calendar days. Mandatory travel insurance with minimum €30,000 coverage is required. Passport must be valid for at least 3 months beyond the planned departure date from the Schengen Area.
📌 Key Features
  • Spain is a Schengen Area member — a Spain Schengen visa permits free movement across all 29 Schengen states, making it an ideal European hub for multi-country business trips.
  • Tourism contributes approximately 12% of Spain's GDP, making hospitality and business infrastructure highly developed across major cities.
  • Spain's Civil Aviation Authority meets international aviation safety standards as assessed by the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA).
  • Major cities including Madrid and Barcelona have implemented Low Emission Zones (ZBE); rental vehicles must comply or risk fines when entering city centres.
⚠️ Key Risks
  • Terrorism: Terrorist groups continue plotting possible attacks with little or no warning, targeting tourist locations, transport hubs, hotels, and public events.
  • Petty Crime: Pickpocketing, bag snatching, and car break-ins are very common — particularly in Barcelona and Madrid — and represent the most frequent risk to business travellers.
  • Civil Unrest: Anti-tourism and housing-cost protests occur regularly in major cities and tourist regions; some demonstrations have turned confrontational.
  • Extreme Weather: Summer heatwaves can exceed 40°C (104°F) in southern regions; severe storms, wildfires, and flash flooding are seasonal hazards.
🛡️ Risk Assessment
✊ Civil Unrest Moderate

Demonstrations are common in Spain and may arise in response to political, economic, or social issues, including ongoing anti-tourism and housing-cost protests. In June 2025, the US Embassy issued a demonstration alert warning of anti-tourism protests across Barcelona, San Sebastián, Valencia, and Mallorca. Hundreds of thousands also marched across over 40 Spanish cities in April 2025 over housing costs, with some protests turning confrontational near tourist hotspots.

🔒 Crime Moderate

Violent crime in Spain is rare and the country ranks 25th on the 2025 Global Peace Index. However, petty crime is a significant concern: pickpocketing, bag snatching, and distraction-based theft are very common in tourist areas, on public transport, and at markets. Car break-ins are also frequent, particularly on motorways and in city parking areas.

🎯 Terrorism Moderate

Spain's national terrorism alert level is rated 'High' by Spanish authorities. Terrorist groups continue to plot possible attacks with little or no warning; targets include tourist locations, transportation centres, markets, hotels, restaurants, places of worship, and major public events. Spanish authorities have taken strong preventive measures, including arrests of suspected plotters, but credible intelligence confirms ongoing planning by extremist groups across Europe.

👩 Women's Safety Low

Spain ranks as one of the safest countries in Europe for solo women travellers, with low rates of violent crime. However, travellers should exercise heightened caution after dark, particularly in nightlife areas, due to risks of drink spiking and opportunistic theft. The national helpline 016 operates 24/7 in 53 languages for victims of gender-based violence.

  • Avoid accepting drinks from strangers and never leave your drink unattended in bars or clubs.
  • Stay in well-lit, populated areas at night and use licensed taxis or reputable ride-hailing apps when travelling alone.
🏥 Health Low

Health risks in Spain are comparable to other Western European countries, and no vaccinations are required for entry. The public and private healthcare systems are well-developed, and medical help is generally accessible nationwide, though response times in smaller cities may be longer.

💨 Air Pollution Low

Spain's overall air quality is generally good, with most cities recording AQI levels in the 'Good' or 'Moderate' range. Urban centres such as Barcelona and Madrid experience elevated NO2 and particulate matter levels from vehicle traffic, with pollution peaking in summer due to heat-intensified ozone formation and in winter due to temperature inversions trapping pollutants in valley cities like Granada.

AQI: 20–60 (Good to Moderate) in major cities; occasionally higher in summer · Peak: June to August (ozone/heat) and December to February (particulate matter in inland cities)

🚗 Road Safety Moderate

Road conditions in Spain are generally good, but driving habits differ significantly from those in many countries — traffic is faster, motorbikes frequently weave through lanes, and urban intersections use independent traffic lights on each side. Rural mountain roads in the Pyrenees and on islands require extra caution, and night driving in rural areas carries risks due to poorly marked roads and free-roaming livestock.

🌪️ Natural Disasters Moderate

Spain experiences a range of natural hazards including intense summer heatwaves (temperatures exceeding 40°C in Andalusia), severe thunderstorms with flash flooding common in coastal Catalonia and Valencia, wildfires during June–August, and periodic earthquakes in southern Spain and the Pyrenees. Volcanic activity is a risk in the Canary Islands.

📰 Current Events
Civil Disturbance Anti-Tourism Demonstrations Across Major Spanish Cities Medium

Coordinated anti-tourism protests took place on 15 June 2025 across Barcelona, San Sebastián, Valencia, Mallorca, and other cities, with some protesters using water pistols and smoke devices near tourist hotspots. The US Embassy Madrid issued a formal Demonstration Alert on 13 June 2025 advising travellers to avoid large gatherings. Protests are expected to continue through peak summer season; business travellers should monitor local media and avoid tourist-dense areas during announced demonstrations.

Security US Embassy Worldwide Caution Security Alert – June 22, 2025 Medium

The US Embassy in Spain issued a Worldwide Caution alert on 22 June 2025 citing the Israel-Iran conflict and the potential for demonstrations against US citizens and interests abroad. Travellers are advised to maintain a low profile, avoid US government facilities during protests, and stay alert to their surroundings, particularly near embassies, consulates, and American-branded establishments.

Security Ongoing Elevated Terrorism Threat Level High

Spain's national terrorism alert level remains at 'High', with credible intelligence confirming that extremist groups continue to plan attacks across Europe, including Spain. Targets include tourist sites, transport hubs (airports and train stations), hotels, restaurants, and major public events. The Guardia Civil and Policía Nacional have increased patrols at high-footfall locations including Barcelona's Las Ramblas, Madrid's Puerta del Sol, and Seville's historic centre.

Natural Disaster Summer Wildfire and Extreme Heat Season Commences Medium

Spain entered its high-risk wildfire season in June 2025, with over 16,000 hectares already burned by end of April 2025. Temperatures in southern Spain, particularly Andalusia, are expected to exceed 40°C during peak summer months, posing health risks for outdoor activities and business events. Travellers should stay hydrated, avoid strenuous outdoor exertion during peak afternoon heat, and monitor local alerts for wildfire evacuations in coastal and rural areas.

✅ Recommendations
1
Carry only a photocopy of your passport when sightseeing, and store the original securely in your hotel safe — Spanish law requires ID to be carried but a certified copy is accepted in most situations.
2
Use anti-theft bags or money belts in crowded tourist areas, on public transport, and at markets; never place valuables in back pockets or leave bags unattended on chair backs in restaurants.
3
Avoid demonstrations and large gatherings; monitor the US Embassy Spain alerts page and local Spanish media for protest announcements, particularly in Barcelona, Mallorca, and Madrid.
4
Obtain an International Driving Permit (IDP) before arrival if you plan to drive; confirm rental vehicles carry a Low Emission Zone (ZBE) compliance sticker to avoid fines in Madrid and Barcelona city centres.
5
Ensure corporate travel insurance provides a minimum of €30,000 medical coverage (Schengen requirement); carry your insurer's 24-hour emergency number and save 112 as your single emergency contact in Spain.
6
Book Schengen visa appointments well in advance (at least 6–8 weeks before travel) through BLS International in India; prepare financial proof, confirmed accommodation, return tickets, and travel insurance documentation.
