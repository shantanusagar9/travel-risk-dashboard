Japan is one of Asia's most advanced economies and a global hub for technology, finance, and manufacturing, offering world-class infrastructure and an exceptionally safe environment for business travellers. The country welcomes record numbers of international visitors and is rapidly expanding its digital entry and border management systems, though visa requirements and overtourism management measures are evolving rapidly in 2026.

Visa (Indian Passport): Indian passport holders require a visa to enter Japan — no visa on arrival. Options include a Japan eVISA (single-entry, tourism) applied online, or a traditional sticker visa via VFS Global. Processing takes approximately 5–7 business days. A detailed day-by-day itinerary, 6-month bank statements, confirmed hotel bookings, and return flight tickets are mandatory. Business visas require an invitation letter and letter of guarantee from the Japanese host company. From 2026, mandatory appointments are required at VFS centres in Chennai, Hyderabad, Kochi, and Mumbai — walk-ins are no longer accepted.
📌 Key Features
  • Exceptionally low crime rate — one of the safest countries in the world for business and leisure travellers
  • Highly efficient public transport network including the Shinkansen bullet train connecting major business hubs
  • Record 36 million international visitors in 2024, with tourism taxes and accommodation levies increasing significantly in 2026
  • Indian passport holders require a visa; eVISA is available for short-term tourism and must be applied for before departure
⚠️ Key Risks
  • Seismic activity — Japan sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire with frequent earthquakes and tsunami risk
  • Natural disasters including typhoons (June–October), volcanic eruptions, and landslides
  • Expanding overtourism management measures including new accommodation taxes, higher departure taxes, and crowd-control restrictions at major sites
  • Bear attacks in northern prefectures (Hokkaido, Akita) — a US Embassy advisory remains in effect
🛡️ Risk Assessment
✊ Civil Unrest Low

Violent demonstrations and civil unrest are very rare in Japan. Most protests are peaceful, regulated by government permit, and do not affect business travel or tourism. The US State Department has not issued a Civil Unrest indicator on the Japan travel advisory.

🔒 Crime Low

Japan has one of the world's lowest crime rates. Pickpocketing and petty theft occur rarely, primarily in crowded tourist districts and transport hubs. Illicit narcotics and after-hours bar districts exist in major cities but are largely discreet; foreigners found with illegal narcotics face a permanent entry ban.

🎯 Terrorism Low

Japan faces no documented organised terrorism threat and the US State Department does not flag terrorism as a risk indicator for the country. Lone-assailant attacks have occurred in recent years, including incidents near the US Embassy in Tokyo and political targets, but these remain isolated and rare. Travellers should remain vigilant in crowded public spaces as a general precaution.

Central Tokyo government and diplomatic districtsMajor transport hubs during peak periods
👩 Women's Safety Low

Japan is considered one of the safest countries in the world for female travellers. However, crowded trains can present a risk of groping (chikan), and women-only train carriages operate during peak hours on all major railway lines as a preventive measure. Solo female business travellers generally report feeling safe across all major cities.

  • Use women-only train carriages during peak hours, available on major rail lines in Tokyo, Osaka, and other cities
  • Avoid poorly lit areas late at night in entertainment districts such as Kabukicho (Shinjuku) and Dotonbori (Osaka)
🏥 Health Low

Japan has a world-class healthcare system with internationally accredited hospitals available in all major cities. Norovirus outbreaks have been reported periodically in popular tourist areas, and travellers should maintain strict hand hygiene, particularly in densely populated areas. Tap water is safe to drink throughout Japan.

💨 Air Pollution Low

Japan's air quality is generally good by global standards. Tokyo's annual average PM2.5 was approximately 9 µg/m³ in 2024, placing it in the 'Good' AQI category. Pollution can spike temporarily in summer months due to heat, industrial emissions, and Gobi Desert dust crossing from mainland Asia.

AQI: 20–85 (typically 'Good' to 'Moderate') · Peak: February–April (Asian dust season), July–August (summer heat and ozone)

🚗 Road Safety Low

Japan has well-maintained roads and strict traffic law enforcement, resulting in one of the lowest road fatality rates in Asia. Driving is on the left side; international driving permits are accepted but self-drive is rarely necessary given the excellence of public transport.

🌪️ Natural Disasters High

Japan is one of the world's most seismically active nations, sitting on the boundary of four tectonic plates, and experiences approximately 1,500 tremors annually. Typhoon season runs from June to October, and multiple active volcanoes including Sakurajima (Alert Level 3) and Shinmoedake (Alert Level 2) currently have no-entry zones. Travellers should download the JNTO Safety Tips app for real-time earthquake, tsunami, and weather alerts.

📰 Current Events
Regulatory Japan Departure Tax Triples to JPY 3,000 from July 2026 Low

Japan's international departure tax will triple from JPY 1,000 to JPY 3,000 per person effective July 1, 2026, automatically included in airline and cruise ticket prices. Kyoto has also introduced Japan's highest-ever hotel accommodation tax of up to JPY 10,000 per person per night for luxury stays, effective March 2026. Business travellers should review and update travel budgets and expense policies accordingly.

Security US Embassy Bear Attack Advisory — Hokkaido & Northern Japan Medium

A US Embassy advisory remains in effect following a surge in bear attacks across northern Japan, with over 100 injuries and at least 13 deaths reported since April 2025, particularly around Sapporo (Hokkaido), Akita, and Iwate prefectures. Authorities have temporarily closed parks and public areas upon sightings. Travellers visiting or transiting northern Japan for business should avoid forested and semi-urban fringe areas and follow local authority guidance.

Natural Disaster Seismic Activity & Megaquake Awareness — Northern Japan Medium

A magnitude 7.5 earthquake struck off the coast of Aomori in December 2025, triggering temporary tsunami warnings (subsequently lifted) and a rare government megaquake advisory. Japan remains at elevated seismic awareness, with the Japan Meteorological Agency noting ongoing risk in the Chishima Trench region near Hokkaido. All travellers should familiarise themselves with earthquake safety protocols and ensure hotel evacuation routes are identified upon check-in.

Regulatory Overtourism Measures — Crowd Controls at Major Sites Low

Japan is implementing broad overtourism countermeasures across Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka in 2026, including entrance fees at popular landmarks, crowd caps on hiking trails, restrictions on street photography in Gion (Kyoto), and stricter Shinkansen reservation requirements. Business travellers should pre-book all transport and accommodation well in advance, particularly during cherry blossom (late March–April) and autumn foliage (November) seasons when occupancy exceeds 90% in major cities.
